<h1><strong>Why Data Privacy Matters Now More Than Ever</strong></h1>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Data privacy matters more now than at any point in the past two decades because breaches keep hitting record levels, scams cost Americans billions, and AI makes both attacks and data collection faster and cheaper.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Your name, phone number, location, and habits now sit in dozens of company databases, and you control very little of what happens to them.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This article explains what is driving the shift, what the latest numbers say, and the practical steps that protect you, whether you're a student, a parent, a remote worker, or a small business owner.</span></p>
<h2><strong>The Short Version</strong></h2>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">IBM's 2026 report puts the global average cost of a data breach at a record </span><strong>$4.99 million</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Identity Theft Resource Center (ITRC) tracked a record </span><strong>3,322 U.S. data compromises</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in 2025.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Consumers reported </span><strong>$15.9 billion</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in fraud losses to the FTC in 2025, up from more than $12 billion in 2024.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Twenty U.S. states now have comprehensive privacy laws, but there is still no comprehensive federal one.</span></li>
</ul>
<h2><strong>What Data Privacy Actually Means</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Data privacy is about control: who collects your personal information, what they do with it, who else gets it, and how long they keep it.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Data security is the related job of protecting that information from theft. You need both. A company can run excellent security and still sell or share your data in ways you never expected.</span></p>

<h3><strong>What IBM Found</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">According to a summary of IBM's 2026 report, the global average breach cost reached $4.99 million, the highest in the report's history.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In the United States, the average hit $11.5 million. Organizations needed an average of 247 days to identify and contain a breach, which reversed several years of improvement.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI plays a growing role. The report found that one in four malicious breaches was AI-enabled, a 56 percent increase from the prior year, and those incidents cost about $6 million on average.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Phishing stayed the leading way in, and voice and SMS phishing carried the highest average cost of any attack vector at $5.29 million.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Healthcare remained the most expensive sector for the thirteenth year in a row at $6.64 million.</span></p>
<h3><strong>What the ITRC Found</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The ITRC's 2025 Annual Data Breach Report counted 3,322 U.S. data compromises, a 5 percent increase from 2024 and a 79 percent jump over five years.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Here, a "compromise" can mean a breach, an accidental exposure, or a leak of data stolen earlier.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Victim notices fell sharply, from about 1.37 billion in 2024 to roughly 279 million in 2025, because the year had no mega-breaches like those that inflated 2024.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Even so, the report found that 70 percent of breach notices did not explain what kind of attack occurred, up from 45 percent in 2023.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Financial services was the most breached industry with 739 compromises, followed by healthcare with 534.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The human side matters too. In an ITRC survey, 88 percent of people who received a breach notice reported at least one negative consequence, such as more spam and phishing or an attempted account takeover.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The ITRC also reported that 81 percent of small businesses experienced a cyberattack, a data breach, or both in the past year.</span></p>
<h3><strong>What the FTC Found</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In testimony delivered on March 25, 2026, the FTC said consumers filed 3 million fraud reports and</span><a href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-testifies-joint-economic-committee-agencys-efforts-combat-fraud"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">reported $15.9 billion in losses</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in 2025, up from 2.6 million reports and more than $12 billion in 2024. Imposter scams were the most frequently reported type, with more than 1 million reports and $3.5 billion in losses.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Investment scams caused the largest losses at $7.9 billion. The agency noted that reported losses represent only part of the true total, since many victims never file a report.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Why Privacy Matters More Than Ever</strong></h2>
<h3><strong>Your Data Lives in More Places</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Every app, store loyalty program, school portal, and smart device holds a slice of your information.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">More copies mean more chances for a leak, and you can't fix a leak at a company you've forgotten you signed up with.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Stolen Data Gets Reused</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Old data stays dangerous. The ITRC noted that one of the biggest incidents in early 2025 involved repackaged data from a 2021 attack on AT&amp;T, which affected nearly 44 million people. Names, phone numbers, and addresses don't expire the way passwords can.</span></p>
<h3><strong>AI Makes Scams More Convincing</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">IBM's numbers show AI-enabled attacks growing quickly. That includes believable phishing messages and cloned voices. Scammers use details from breached data to make their messages sound personal, which raises the odds that you'll act on them.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Companies Often Say Too Little</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">When 70 percent of breach notices skip the attack details, you can't judge your own risk. That puts the burden on you to act before you know the full story.</span></p>
<h2><strong>The Rules Differ by Where You Live</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The United States still has no comprehensive federal privacy law. Instead,</span><a href="https://multistate.us/insider/2026/2/4/all-of-the-comprehensive-privacy-laws-that-take-effect-in-2026"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">20 states have comprehensive privacy laws</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in effect, and Indiana, Kentucky, and Rhode Island joined the list on January 1, 2026.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Many of these laws give residents rights such as accessing, deleting, or opting out of the sale of their data, but the details differ from state to state.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If you live in a state without such a law, you have fewer legal tools.</span></p>
<h2><strong>What You Stand to Lose</strong></h2>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Money.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Fraud losses reached $15.9 billion in 2025 by FTC reports alone.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Identity.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Static details like your name, date of birth, and address can fuel identity theft for years.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Time and peace of mind.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> The ITRC survey found that nearly nine in ten notice recipients faced at least one negative consequence.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Control.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Pew Research Center's October 2023 survey found that 56 percent of Americans frequently click "agree" on privacy policies without reading them, so most of us give away rights without realizing it.</span></li>
</ul>
<h2><strong>How to Protect Your Privacy Today</strong></h2>
<ol>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Use a unique password for every account.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> The ITRC recommends this because one breach shouldn't unlock everything else. A password manager makes it practical.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Turn on multi-factor authentication.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Choose an authenticator app or a passkey over text messages when you can.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Slow down on urgent calls and texts.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> IBM found voice and SMS phishing to be the costliest attack type, so verify any "urgent" request by contacting the company directly.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Review app permissions.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Remove location, microphone, and contact access that an app doesn't need.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Delete accounts you no longer use.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Fewer accounts mean fewer places for your data to leak from.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Keep devices updated.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Updates close known security holes.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Read breach notices and act on them.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Change passwords, watch your accounts, and consider a credit freeze if you're in the U.S.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Report scams.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> File a report at</span><a href="https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">ReportFraud.ftc.gov</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> so the FTC can track and act on patterns.</span></li>
</ol>

<h2><strong>Frequently Asked Questions</strong></h2>
<h3><strong>Why is data privacy important?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It gives you control over how companies and others use your personal information, and it reduces your risk of fraud, identity theft, and unwanted tracking.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Is data privacy the same as data security?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">No. Privacy covers how your data is collected, used, and shared. Security covers how well it is protected from theft. Strong protection needs both.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Does a VPN make me completely private?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">No. A VPN hides your IP address and encrypts your traffic, but it can't prevent phishing, stop apps from collecting data you share, or undo a company's breach.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Are data breaches getting worse?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">By count, yes. The ITRC tracked a record 3,322 U.S. compromises in 2025, though the number of victim notices dropped after the mega-breaches of 2024.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Does the U.S. have a federal privacy law?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Not a comprehensive one. Twenty states have their own comprehensive laws, so your rights depend on where you live.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Start With Three Small Changes Today</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">You don't have to overhaul your digital life in one afternoon. Switch on multi-factor authentication for your email and bank, install a password manager, and delete three accounts you no longer use. Those steps take less than an hour and cut your exposure where it counts.</span></p>
